# Common bean disease symptoms across Africa

Angular leaf spot, caused by the fungus Pseudocercospora griseola, is
one of the most widely distributed bean diseases in Africa. It causes
angular, grey-to-brown necrotic spots on leaves that follow the leaf
veins, giving them their characteristic angular shape, and can also
affect pods. It spreads fastest in humid conditions. Management
includes resistant varieties, crop rotation away from beans for at
least one season, and fungicide application if pressure is severe.

Anthracnose, caused by Colletotrichum lindemuthianum, shows as dark,
sunken lesions on pods, often with a slightly raised black ring and a
reddish-brown to cinnamon-colored border. On leaves, infected veins
develop elongated, angular, brick-red to purple spots that darken to
black over time. It spreads through infected seed and is worsened by
splashing water (rain or overhead irrigation) carrying spores between
plants. Using certified disease-free seed is the single most effective
prevention step.

Bean rust, caused by Uromyces appendiculatus, produces small
reddish-brown raised pustules scattered across the leaf surface,
giving leaves a rusty, powdery appearance when pustules rupture and
release spores. It spreads via wind-blown spores and is favored by
moderate temperatures with high humidity.

Common bacterial blight and halo blight are also widespread; both
cause water-soaked spots on leaves that dry into brown, often
yellow-haloed lesions, and both spread through infected seed and
splashing water, making seed source the key prevention point for
both.
