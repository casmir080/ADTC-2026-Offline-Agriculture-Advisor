# Common bean general agronomy notes

Common bean is a major protein and income source for smallholder
farmers, often intercropped with maize. Most of its major diseases
(anthracnose, common bacterial blight, halo blight) are seed-borne,
which makes seed source the single highest-leverage decision a farmer
makes for disease prevention, often more impactful than in-season
fungicide use.

Beans are more sensitive to waterlogging than maize or cassava and
perform poorly in poorly-drained soils. Crop rotation with non-legume
crops (such as maize) for at least one season helps break disease
cycles for soil-borne and residue-borne pathogens between bean
plantings on the same plot.
