# Cassava mosaic disease and cassava brown streak disease

Cassava mosaic disease (CMD) is the most widespread viral disease of
cassava in Sub-Saharan Africa. Symptoms include mosaic and mottled
patterns on leaves, misshapen and twisted leaflets, and an overall
reduction in leaf and plant size. Severely affected plants produce few
or no usable tuberous roots. CMD spreads two ways: through whitefly
insects that carry the virus between plants, and through infected stem
cuttings, since cassava is normally propagated by planting cuttings
rather than seed. Management centers on using disease-free planting
material (clean cuttings from healthy plants), planting resistant
varieties where available, removing and destroying severely infected
plants to reduce the source of infection, and controlling whitefly
populations.

Cassava brown streak disease is a separate but equally serious viral
disease, also spread by whiteflies. Leaf symptoms can be subtle or
absent even when the disease is present. The most damaging symptom is
a dry, brown, corky necrosis inside the tuberous roots, which can make
the harvest unusable even when the plant looked healthy above ground.
Because root symptoms aren't visible until harvest, sourcing cuttings
only from fields with a known clean history is especially important
for this disease.
