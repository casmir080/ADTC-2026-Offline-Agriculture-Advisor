# Cassava general agronomy notes

Cassava is propagated from stem cuttings rather than seed, so the
health of the "mother" plant directly determines whether the next
planting starts disease-free. Cuttings should be taken from
vigorous, visibly healthy plants, ideally from fields with no recent
history of mosaic or brown streak symptoms.

Cassava tolerates drought and poor soils better than maize or beans,
which is part of why it's a common food-security crop in marginal
growing areas. However, it is highly sensitive to viral diseases
spread by whiteflies, which can spread faster in hot, dry conditions
that favor whitefly population growth.

Harvest timing is flexible since roots can often be left in the
ground for many months past maturity, but leaving diseased plants in
the field longer also extends the period they can serve as a source
of infection for whiteflies to spread to neighboring plants.
