# Maize nutrient deficiency symptoms

Nitrogen deficiency in maize shows first as yellowing that starts at the
tip of older, lower leaves and moves down the midrib in a V-shaped
pattern, while the leaf edges stay green longer. Plants are stunted and
pale overall. This is the most common deficiency in smallholder maize
fields with limited fertilizer use.

Phosphorus deficiency shows as a purplish or reddish discoloration on
leaves, most visible on younger plants, along with stunted growth and
delayed maturity. It is more common in cold, wet soils early in the
season and in highly weathered acidic soils.

Potassium deficiency causes yellowing and browning that starts at the
leaf margins and tip, not between the veins, often giving a scorched
look to older leaves. Stalks may also be weaker and more prone to
lodging (falling over).

Zinc deficiency, common in some high-pH or sandy soils, shows as broad
pale or white striping between the veins on younger leaves, sometimes
called "white bud," along with shortened internodes.

Before applying any fertilizer, a soil test is the most reliable way to
confirm which nutrient is actually short, since several deficiencies
produce visually similar yellowing.
