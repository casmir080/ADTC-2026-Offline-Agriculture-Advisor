# Maize pests and diseases common across Sub-Saharan Africa

Fall armyworm is one of the most damaging maize pests across the
continent since its arrival around 2016. Early signs are small,
"window-pane" translucent patches on young leaves where larvae have
fed, followed by larger ragged holes and visible dark green to brown
frass (droppings) inside the whorl. Eggs are laid in fuzzy white
masses on the underside of leaves. Early detection and handpicking of
egg masses is effective if caught early. Biological controls and
targeted biopesticides are preferred over broad-spectrum synthetic
insecticides, since fall armyworm has developed resistance to several
chemical classes in some regions.

Maize streak virus, transmitted by leafhoppers, causes narrow yellow
or white streaks running parallel to the leaf veins, with affected
plants often severely stunted if infected young. There is no cure once
infected. Management focuses on prevention: planting resistant or
tolerant maize varieties, early planting to avoid peak leafhopper
periods, and removing volunteer maize plants that can host the virus
between seasons.

Maize stalk borer larvae tunnel into stems, creating visible holes and
weakening stalks so they break or lodge in wind. Look for "shot-hole"
patterns in young whorl leaves before tunneling becomes visible on the
stalk itself.
